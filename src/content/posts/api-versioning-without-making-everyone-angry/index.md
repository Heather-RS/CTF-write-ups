# Comparative analysis – finding out where we went wrong and what we got right

# Preamble -
My team attended defcom 34 as librenovus and places 72nd in the ctf we were all news to OSINT and were a team that had never met each other until this event occurred.
This CTF analysis is to find out where we went wrong to improve on for later, as a comparison we are using an article written by the all women based team that won called oSInt & Giggles.

# First things first – what techniques did we actually use?
Looking at what the leading team did we did follow with a few things, username pivots, nicknames, I think every team starts off this way especially with social media, so we at least had that under wraps, what we had not fully utilised was google dorking or search engine techniques, while we did use some techniques it was sparse or inconsistent depending on the individual and their experience level. Something the lead team and others had done. This is not a failure on our part of mostly inexperience in using these day to day.

# The actual cases and what we did right and what could be improved
Looking at their first assessment of their cases for the most recent missing person, this case I was assigned on. Everyone had different cases, but here is the point I think we missed. We did not work together as well as we could have, not until the last stretch of the CTF and we did not pivot often either. I think this caused some level of information disconnect leading to confusion and frustration when we did work together on another case. Again not so much a failure, but a lack of experience that we had, team work is often important in missing cases; so gathering info and collecting clues together, keeps the flow of information neater and easier to understand. 

Now onto my personal analysis as I also worked on the most recent person, I know that personally I had used similar techniques. And I also ran into similar problems, what the lead team had done though was simple was effective, and that was use date and time in search engines to find more information before the disappearance only focusing on info after the case had happened. This is where I had missed out on vital context and clues.

The other techniques that the lead team had used for social media pivoting and searching profiles was something my team had done well. Though we worked as individuals we did provide feedback to each other on what we were doing and as a result we were able to start using similar techniques on the profiles we researched. Such techniques were reviewing social media profiles, discovering alternative names, finding useful pivots from the names, locations and profiles found, using text messages from the social media profiles to connect info, such as relationships and family members and finding court cases/public info to find further connections. 
Another technique that the lead team had used that we didn’t was using less common family names to find connections, I think in the future this is a good technique to have to find out more about a person while cutting out the noise. Court records was another thing we used, but I think we did not find as much as the lead team, so maybe its something our team could look into more in the future.
One interesting thing that I believe we did miss was the maiden name of the one of the missing people, the lead team had found this but we had not, so it shows that we may not have been going into the profiles as deeply as we could, another consequence of breaking up the work load of missing people to individuals rather than working together.
My final conclusions echo what Amy E. of oSInt & Giggles, so I’ll let her words ring true.
I have emphasized throughout my years of teaching OSINT: tools matter, but methodology matters more. We did not use paid tools. Our results came from Boolean searches, Google dorking, knowing how to pivot from the information we had, determining what was relevant, validating connections, and deciding where to spend our limited time. Just as important was recognizing when to dig deeper and when to cut our losses before a rabbit hole consumed too much time.
- Amy, E.
